00

Regulatory foundation

What's actually verified below, what's a reasonable default pending confirmation, and the one design principle every other section in this document follows from.

What's verified vs. assumed. PCN's framework is confirmed from current reporting: PCN replaced its 2021 Online Pharmacy Regulations with the Electronic Pharmacy Regulations 2026, which this document treats as the applicable rulebook. No VCN-specific rule on telemedicine or remote prescribing was found in this research — the veterinary sections below carry the international default (no diagnosis or prescription without a physical exam) as a safe assumption, flagged for direct VCN confirmation in section 11. Treat every specific obligation below as a starting point for legal review against the regulators' own published text, not as that text itself.

What changed, and why it matters here

FindingWhat it means for Robakeye
PCN's 2021 rules barred online pharmacies from dispensing prescriptions that arose from an online consultation.This is the line this whole document is built around. A prescription Robakeye dispenses against must come from a prescriber elsewhere (a doctor, a hospital) — the "Talk to a Pharmacist" flow in §03 is advisory and triage only, and must never itself generate the authorization to purchase a POM.

The one rule that shapes every flow below

What Robakeye does

  • Verifies a prescription that already exists, written by a doctor or (for animals) a vet elsewhere.
  • Lets a pharmacist counsel, triage, and refer.
  • Dispenses OTC medicines freely.
  • Books in-person vet visits where diagnosis and prescribing actually happen.

What Robakeye never does

  • Has a pharmacist issue a new prescription from a chat or callback.
  • Sells controlled substances online, under any flow.
  • Lets a vet diagnose or prescribe without a physical exam of the animal.
  • Treats an OCR-extracted drug name as a substitute for pharmacist review.
01

Product classification system

Three tiers, not two. The brief's addition of Controlled substances as a non-sellable third class is the most consequential change from the earlier IA spec's binary OTC/prescription model.

Who sets the tierA pharmacist-managed field on the product record, not a one-time import flag — a drug's classification can change if regulation changes, and the product page must reflect that on the next read, not require a redeploy.

Controlled substances need their own admin guardrail. Because there's no UI path to purchase, the only way a Controlled item could become purchasable is a data error — someone misclassifying it as POM during catalogue entry. The product-edit screen in admin should require a second pharmacist's sign-off before a Controlled item can be reclassified downward, the same two-person-rule pattern banks use for high-risk changes.

02

Prescription verification workflow

The IA spec's prescription flow treated approval as one decision per request. Real prescriptions carry several drugs — a pharmacist needs to approve two and query one, not reject the whole thing over a single unclear line.

    B --> C["Pharmacist opens the request\nin the review queue"]
    C --> D["Reviews each line item\nseparately against the image"]
    D --> E{"Per line item:\napprove / query / reject"}
    E -- "approve" --> F["Line enters 'approved' state\ntime-limited unlock token issued for that line only"]
    E -- "query" --> G["Line enters 'query' state\ncustomer notified, asked for clarification\n(e.g. re-upload, call the branch)"]
    E -- "reject" --> H["Line enters 'rejected' state\ncustomer notified with a stated reason"]
    F --> I["Customer sees a per-line result:\nitems ready to buy, items needing action"]
    G --> I
    H --> I

  • 1Status badge covers the whole request; each line below carries its own independent status.
  • 2Approve generates that line's own unlock token — the customer can buy the amoxicillin even while line 3 is still rejected.
  • 3Query is distinct from reject: it's recoverable without a new submission if the pharmacist can read a re-upload of just the unclear part.
  • 4A pharmacist spotting a Controlled item on an uploaded prescription rejects that line outright — it was never purchasable through this flow regardless of what the prescription says.
  • 5Every decision auto-attaches both the individual reviewer and the Superintendent Pharmacist of record — the audit trail answers "who personally decided" and "who's accountable for the operation" separately.

What the customer sees

Approved line

  • "Ready to buy" badge, add-to-cart unlocked.
  • Countdown showing the unlock window remaining.

Queried line

  • Stated reason, plain language ("dosage unclear, please re-upload").
  • One-tap re-upload for just that line.

Rejected line

  • Stated reason — never a bare "rejected" with no explanation.
  • If the reason is regulatory (e.g. Controlled), a note to visit a branch in person, not a retry prompt.
03

Talk to a Pharmacist

Advisory and triage, never prescribing. This flow can recommend a visit, explain a dosage, or flag an interaction — it cannot be the thing that makes a POM purchasable.

flowchart TD
    A["Customer opens Talk to a Pharmacist"] --> B{"Within business hours?"}
    B -- "yes" --> C["Live chat — joins a queue\nif no pharmacist is free"]
    B -- "no" --> D["Callback request form\nname, phone, question, preferred time"]
    C --> E["Pharmacist responds"]
    D --> F["Pharmacist calls back next business day\n(or sooner, per branch SLA)"]
    A --> G["WhatsApp Business API\nalternative channel, same queue"]
    G --> E
    E --> H{"Outcome"}
    F --> H
    H -- "advisory only" --> I["Interaction record logged:\ndate, duration, topic category, outcome\n— not necessarily a full transcript"]
    H -- "customer has an existing\nPOM prescription to fill" --> J["Routed to the upload flow — §02\nnever authorized from the chat itself"]
    H -- "needs an in-person assessment" --> K["Routed to visit a branch"]
    I --> L[("Consultation log")]
    J --> L
    K --> L

Logging, scoped for privacy

What's logged

  • Date, start/end time, channel (chat/WhatsApp/callback).
  • A topic category the pharmacist selects (e.g. "dosage question," "side effect," "OTC recommendation").
  • Outcome (advice given / routed to upload flow / routed to branch).
  • Which pharmacist handled it.

What's not logged by default

  • Full message transcripts — the brief explicitly calls this out as unnecessary for the business record the audit trail needs.
  • Health details beyond the topic category, unless the customer's own account already holds them (e.g. a stated allergy, §04).

This is a narrower retention policy than the prescription audit trail in §02, which does need the full decision record — the two serve different purposes. A consult is advice; a prescription decision is a dispensing record. Confirm this distinction against NDPA data-minimisation guidance and PCN's own record-keeping requirement before launch (§11).

04

Interaction & allergy warnings future phase

A basic, rule-based safety net — explicitly not a substitute for the pharmacist review in §02.

How it works

  • Customer optionally records known allergies on their profile (free text + a controlled list of common ones).
  • When 2+ medications are in the cart together, or a cart item matches a stated allergy, a rule-based check runs against a maintained interaction/allergen table.
  • A match shows a warning banner in the cart — not a hard block, since the pharmacist review for any POM line is the actual safety gate.

Guardrails

  • The warning's copy states plainly that it is not a substitute for pharmacist advice and recommends using "Talk to a Pharmacist" (§03).
  • The interaction table needs a named pharmacist owner who maintains it — an unmaintained rule set is worse than none, since it implies a completeness the system doesn't have.
  • OTC-only interactions still route through this check; it isn't limited to POM items.

Scoped as future-phase per the brief. If built, treat it as advisory UI only — never as a basis for auto-approving or auto-blocking a line in the §02 review queue.

07

Pet records & vaccination reminders

Every visit appends to the pet's record. Vaccination due-dates turn that record into a recurring reason to come back.

flowchart LR
    A["Vet completes a visit"] --> B["Appends to pet record:\ndiagnosis notes, treatment,\nvaccination given, next due date"]
    B --> C[("Pet profile\nmedical history + vaccination log")]
    C --> D["Scheduled job checks\nupcoming due dates daily"]
    D --> E{"Due date within\nreminder window? (e.g. 2 weeks)"}
    E -- "yes" --> F["SMS/email:\n'Bingo's rabies vaccine is due in 2 weeks — Book a visit'"]
    E -- "no" --> D
    F --> G["Deep-links straight into\nBook a Visit, pet + service pre-filled"]

Pet profile fields

  • Name, species, breed, DOB/estimated age, photo.
  • Known conditions (owner-entered).
  • Linked to one owner account; one account can hold several pets.

Per-visit record entry

  • Diagnosis notes, written by the attending vet, customer-visible.
  • Treatment prescribed — if it's a POM, links into §02's verification flow rather than being separately authorized.
  • Vaccination given + the computed next-due date that drives §07's reminder job.

The reminder is the retention mechanism the brief names explicitly — it only works if every visit's vaccination entry reliably writes a next-due date. Make that field required on the vet's visit-closing form, not optional, or the reminder job silently has nothing to check.

08

Veterinary product catalogue

The same three-tier logic from §01, applied to animals — most pet products are open, but pet medications get the same gate human POMs do.

Freely purchasable

  • Leashes, cages, bowls, grooming brushes, soap/shampoo, toys, bedding.
  • Standard e-commerce flow, no restriction — identical to a Supermarket item.

Vet-verification required

  • Pet medications flagged the same way human POMs are — locked "Add to cart," routed to a parallel verification queue.
  • Reviewed against a vet-issued prescription (not a human doctor's), by a VCN-registered vet, same append-only audit pattern as §02.

This is a second, parallel review queue — not the same queue as §02's pharmacist queue. A pet antibiotic needs a vet's sign-off, not a pharmacist's; the admin dashboard in §09 keeps the two queues visibly separate so staff never work the wrong one.

Assumptions & open items

What this document couldn't verify directly, and who needs to answer it before build.

  1. VCN's actual position on remote diagnosis/prescribingNo Nigeria-specific source was found. This document applies the international default (physical exam required) as a precaution. Get this confirmed directly from VCN or veterinary counsel — it's the single biggest open question in the veterinary half of this document.
  2. Exact text of the PCN Electronic Pharmacy Regulations 2026This document is built from secondary reporting (news coverage and legal-review articles), not the primary regulation text. Obtain and review the official PCN publication before finalising §00's obligations, especially the online-consultation restriction and the National Electronic Pharmacy Platform integration.
  3. Prescription and consultation-log retention periodsFlagged already in the IA and technical architecture specs — needs a confirmed number of years from PCN guidance, which then sets the S3 lifecycle policy and the NDPA-minimisation review for §03's consultation logs.
  4. Minor-ailment or pharmacist-prescribing schemesSome jurisdictions let registered pharmacists prescribe a narrow list of items themselves (e.g. certain antibiotics for simple UTIs). Confirm whether Nigeria's framework includes anything similar — if so, §01's classification table may need a fourth tier.
  5. Real service prices, durations, and prerequisites§05's table is sample data for layout. Needs the flagship clinic's actual price list and vet staff's input on realistic slot lengths and buffers.
  6. Superintendent Pharmacist's specific legal responsibilitiesThis document treats the role as "named and registered," per secondary reporting — the exact scope of their accountability (e.g. do they personally co-sign every rejection, or only hold overall responsibility) needs direct PCN confirmation.
