Revenue feature roadmap · v1.0

Beyond core commerce

Twelve features that drive revenue once the core platform (catalogue, cart, checkout, the three verticals) is live — prioritised by impact against effort, and sequenced into MVP, Phase 2 and Phase 3. Several lean on infrastructure the earlier specs already built, which is where the cheapest wins are.

MVP
5 features, ship at or near launch
Phase 2
6 features, need volume or data first
Phase 3
2 features, new business models
00

Strategic framing

Three ideas carry the whole prioritisation, not just gut feel per feature.

Reuse beats new infrastructure

The technical architecture doc already has Reviews, Promotions, the order calculation engine, and SMS/email providers. A feature that's mostly new UI on existing data (reviews, flash sales, the delivery progress bar) is cheap. A feature needing a new ledger, a new billing relationship, or a new approval workflow (subscriptions, gift cards, B2B) genuinely isn't.

Some features need volume first

A loyalty tier system with no repeat customers yet, or a segmentation tool with no customer history, optimises nothing. These wait for Phase 2 not because they're hard, but because launch-week Robakeye hasn't generated the data they act on.

Compliance already drew some lines

Prescription Refill Reminders re-triggers the same pharmacist verification flow from the compliance doc — it's a new entry point into an existing, already-regulated process, not a new one. That containment is what keeps its effort moderate instead of high.

02

MVP features

High impact, low effort — mostly new UI layered on infrastructure the other specs already built. Ship at or shortly after launch.

Abandoned cart recovery I 8 · E 3

  • 1h / 24h / 72h SMS+email sequence, the last one carrying a small incentive code.
  • Needs only a scheduled job reading cart-created-but-not-ordered state and the existing Termii/SendGrid integrations (technical architecture doc §05) — no new provider.

Flash sales & time-limited deals I 6 · E 3

  • The homepage's combo-deal countdown banner (homepage spec §07) already has the UI; this is admin scheduling plus a stock-threshold "Only 5 left" read from branch_inventory.
  • Reuses the Promotions module's date-range and usage-limit fields (admin dashboard spec §06) — a flash sale is a promo with a short window, not a new entity.

Reviews & social proof I 7 · E 3

  • The Reviews table and POST /v1/products/:id/reviews endpoint already exist (technical architecture doc §02–03).
  • Adds: a post-delivery automated request, a rating/count on product cards, and a small loyalty-point bonus for a photo review — the last one is a stub until Loyalty ships in Phase 2, but the review itself doesn't need to wait.

Cart free-delivery progress bar I 6 · E 2

  • Pulled out of the brief's "Cross-sell & upsell engine" as its own line because it's nearly free: a bar reading the free-delivery threshold already in the order calculation engine (technical architecture doc §04), no recommendation logic at all.
  • The "frequently bought together" and "also bought" widgets are the real engineering lift — those ship in Phase 2 under their own entry.

Pet health content hub I 5 · E 3

  • A blog is mostly a CMS content type plus category/tag pages — low engineering effort relative to the rest of this list.
  • Worth starting early specifically because SEO is slow-compounding: the earlier the content exists, the more it's indexed by the time Robakeye needs the organic traffic.
03

Phase 2 features

High impact, but either genuinely more effort to build correctly, or features that need real customer volume to have anything to act on.

Loyalty / rewards programme I 8 · E 6

  • Points ledger, configurable earn rate, Bronze/Silver/Gold tiers, referral codes (both sides get ₦500 off) — several new entities and a redemption step inside the order calculation engine's existing loyalty-discount line (technical architecture doc §04, step 4, already reserved for this).
  • Needs repeat-purchase volume to be worth the build — a tier system with no second orders yet optimises nothing.

Prescription refill reminders I 8 · E 5

  • Infers a refill cycle from order history per chronic-medication SKU, then fires a reminder with one-click reorder.
  • Reorder re-enters the existing per-line prescription verification flow (compliance doc §02) if the unlock token has expired — this is a new trigger into regulated infrastructure that already exists, not a new compliance surface.

Subscribe & save I 7 · E 7

  • Recurring Paystack billing, frequency selection, pre-charge notification, pause/cancel — real new billing-relationship complexity, including handling a failed recurring charge gracefully.
  • A subscribed POM item still has to pass prescription verification on each cycle, same reasoning as refill reminders, compounding the effort here specifically.

Cross-sell recommendation widgets I 7 · E 5

  • "Frequently bought together" can launch as admin-curated pairs via the existing combo-deal mechanism (admin dashboard spec §06) — low effort.
  • "Customers who bought this also bought" needs real co-purchase data, which is exactly the volume problem — it has nothing to compute from on day one.

Customer segmentation for marketing I 7 · E 6

  • A segment builder (e.g. "pet owners, no order in 60 days") over the existing customer/order schema, feeding Termii/email campaigns.
  • Same volume dependency as Loyalty — segmenting a small, new customer base returns segments too small to target usefully.

Multi-branch fulfilment optimisation I 6 · E 6

  • A transfer-suggestion report reading branch_inventory and inventory_log (technical architecture doc §02) across branches — the data already exists from day one.
  • The effort is mostly in the suggestion logic itself; the real-world payoff needs a few months of demand pattern to be reliable, which is why it's Phase 2 rather than MVP despite low new-infrastructure cost.

Gift cards I 5 · E 5

  • A stored-value ledger, purchase and redemption flows, breakage/fraud handling — genuinely new money-holding infrastructure, not a UI layer on something else.
  • Useful mainly as an acquisition channel (gifting brings in a new customer) — worth doing, not worth doing before the retention features above it.
04

Phase 3 features

Lower impact relative to their effort today, or genuinely a different business model from the consumer storefront the rest of this series specs.

B2B / bulk ordering portal I 5 · E 8

  • Named "future phase consideration" in the brief itself. Separate pricing tier, invoice-based payment (a real departure from the Paystack-only consumer checkout), and likely its own approval workflow for an account, not an individual.
  • The addressable segment (hotels, clinics, offices) is real but small relative to the consumer verticals already being built — worth a dedicated spec of its own when pursued, not a bolt-on.

Gift cards, reconsidered timing see note

  • Plotted in Phase 2 on effort alone (§03) — listed again here because its actual priority depends on a business call, not engineering: is acquisition-via-gifting more valuable right now than the retention features ahead of it in the queue? Default assumption below is no, until MVP's retention features (Reviews, Abandoned Cart) show results worth compounding further.
05

Sequencing & dependencies

A few features genuinely gate others — building them out of order means rework.

DependencyWhy it's ordered this way
Reviews (MVP) → photo-review loyalty bonus (Phase 2)The review mechanism ships first and works standalone; the points bonus is just a hook added once Loyalty exists, not a reason to delay Reviews.
Order history volume → Refill Reminders, Segmentation, Multi-branch OptimisationAll three compute something from past orders. None of them are wrong to build early, but none of them are useful until the MVP features have been live long enough to generate that history.
Prescription verification flow (already built) → Refill Reminders, Subscribe & SaveBoth re-enter the same compliance-doc flow for any POM line. Building either before that flow exists would mean building it twice.
Combo-deal mechanism (already built) → curated "frequently bought together"The simple half of Cross-sell ships on existing admin tooling; only the data-driven "also bought" half waits on volume.
Loyalty points ledger → photo-review bonus, tier-based perks referenced elsewhereAny feature that pays out points needs the ledger to exist first — this is the one Phase 2 feature most other Phase 2/3 ideas quietly depend on.
06

Assumptions & open items

Business decisions this roadmap needs, not engineering ones.

  1. Loyalty earn rate and tier thresholds§03 assumes a configurable rate exists but doesn't set one — needs a real ₦-per-point ratio and the order-value thresholds for Bronze/Silver/Gold before the points ledger can be built.
  2. Referral payout mechanism"Both get ₦500 off" needs a decision on whether that's a wallet credit, a single-use promo code, or loyalty points — each has different fraud-prevention requirements (e.g. self-referral abuse).
  3. Subscription failed-payment policy§03 doesn't specify what happens after N failed recurring charges — pause, cancel, or retry on a schedule — needs a business answer before the Subscribe & Save flow can be finished.
  4. Gift card expiry and breakageWhether Robakeye's gift cards expire, and how unredeemed balance is accounted for, is a finance and possibly regulatory question, not just a product one.
  5. B2B pricing model§04 defers the entire B2B portal, but even a future spec needs a starting assumption: percentage discount off standard pricing, or a separate negotiated price list per account.
  6. Impact/effort scores are directional, not measuredEvery score in §01 is this document's own estimate for sequencing purposes, not a result of user research or a cost estimate from engineering — revisit scores once MVP data (actual cart-abandonment rate, actual review submission rate) is in.
