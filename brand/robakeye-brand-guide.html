Typography

Sora for headlines, Work Sans for everything people read closely. Both are free on Google Fonts under the Open Font License.

Headings · Sora
Aa Rr
SemiBold 600Bold 700ExtraBold 800

Geometric and open. It matches the logo, which is drawn in Sora ExtraBold. Use it for headings, navigation, buttons and labels. ExtraBold is for hero headlines only.

Body · Work Sans
Aa ₦
Regular 400Medium 500SemiBold 600Bold 700Italic 500

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz 0123456789 ₦ ẹ ọ ṣ Ẹ Ọ Ṣ à á è é

Highly legible at small sizes, with clear figures for dosages and prices. It includes the naira sign and Yoruba letters.

Sora has no ₦ and no Yoruba dot-below letters (ẹ, ọ, ṣ). Poppins, the other suggested heading font, has the same gap. We checked both font files directly. So the heading stack is "Sora", "Work Sans", sans-serif: if a stray ₦ or ẹ appears in a heading, the browser fills it in from Work Sans instead of a system font. Two rules follow from this. Set all prices in Work Sans Bold, including large ones. Set any heading written mainly in Yoruba entirely in Work Sans Bold, for example: Ẹ kú àbọ̀ sí Robakeye.

On phones

  • Display 2.5rem, H1 2.125rem, H2 1.75rem, H3 1.375rem.
  • H4 and below stay the same size.
  • Use clamp() between the two sizes.

Medical text

  • Never smaller than 1rem (16px), including on labels and leaflets on screen.
  • No italics and no all-caps for dosage or warnings.
  • Put numbers and units in bold: 2 tablets, every 8 hours.

Line length and spacing

  • Keep reading text near 65 characters per line.
  • Use sentence case for headings and buttons.
  • Use letter-spacing only on caps labels.

05

Iconography

Line icons on a 24 px grid, 2 px stroke, round caps and joins. The rounded corners echo the rounded house in the badge.

Canvas24 × 24 px. Export at 24, 32 and 48 px.
Live area20 × 20 px, leaving 2 px padding. Small details like caps and wheels may reach 1 px from the edge.
Stroke2 px, centred, never scaled. Use 1.5 px at 16 px.
CornersRound caps, round joins, 1 to 2 px corner radius on rectangles.
FillNone. Use a soft tinted tile behind the icon for emphasis, not a filled glyph.
ColourOne colour, inherited from the text colour. Navy on light, white on Navy, Lavender Deep in Vet. Never Amber on white.

06

Photography

Real people in real Robakeye spaces. Shoot at the Ologuneru flagship and around Ibadan, not with imported stock. Each module has its own light and mood.

PH · Pharmacy

Clean, bright, clinical

Light
Bright, even daylight (5000 to 5600 K). High key, soft shadows, white backgrounds.
People
Nigerian pharmacists of different ages and genders, in white coats with navy name badges. Customers being counselled at eye level across the counter.
Shot list
Counselling at the counter · a pharmacist checking a prescription · neat shelf fronts · single products on a white sweep · hands passing a labelled bag
Avoid
Spilled pills, needles in close-up, anyone who looks unwell or distressed, readable prescriptions or patient names, foreign stock settings.
VT · Veterinary

Warm, candid, close

Light
Warm window light (about 4000 K), shallow depth of field, natural skin and fur tones.
People
Owners with their pets and Robakeye vet staff in lavender scrubs, inside the Ologuneru flagship. Shoot at the animal's eye level.
Shot list
A vet greeting a dog in the waiting area · a child holding a cat on the consult table · a puppy's first visit · supplies aisle with an owner choosing food
Avoid
Surgery, blood, needles going in, frightened or restrained animals, staged studio pets.
SM · Supermarket

Vibrant, full, local

Light
Bright and saturated, with no colour cast from shop lighting. Correct to neutral white, then lift warmth slightly.
People
Ibadan shoppers: families, students and traders, in everyday clothes. Show real moments like weighing produce or chatting at the till.
Shot list
Full produce displays (tomatoes, peppers, yam, plantain) · overhead basket flat-lays · staff restocking · a shopper at checkout with a branded bag
Avoid
Empty or messy shelves, handwritten price stickers in frame, price claims baked into photos.

Consent

  • Get a signed model release from everyone identifiable.
  • For children, a parent or guardian signs.
  • Pet photos need the owner's permission.
  • Never photograph a customer's prescription, medicine bag label or screen.

Treatment

  • Use the module's grading targets above. Skin tones must look natural; never lighten them.
  • No heavy filters, vignettes or duotones on people.
  • A navy overlay at 60% is allowed behind white headlines.

Crops

  • Hero banners: 16:9 on desktop, 4:5 on mobile. Keep subjects out of the left half so headlines can sit there.
  • Product images: 1:1 on white, product filling 80% of the frame.

Voice and tone

One voice, three moods. Robakeye always sounds like a knowledgeable neighbour: helpful, plain-spoken and never pushy.

PH · Pharmacy
ReassuringAuthoritativeClear

Short sentences, exact numbers, no jargon without an explanation. Point people to the pharmacist whenever a question has a personal answer.

SayNot sure two medicines are safe together? Ask our pharmacist before you take them.
SayTake 1 tablet every 8 hours, after food. Do not take more than 3 in 24 hours.
Don't sayGuaranteed cure for malaria in 24 hours!
Don't sayJust pop 2 and you go dey alright.
VT · Veterinary
FriendlyCaringApproachable

Speak to the owner and use the pet's name. Be gentle about health worries and clear about next steps.

SayBingo's yearly shots are due. Book a time that suits you and we'll keep it calm and quick.
SayNew puppy? Bring them in for a first check-up and we'll talk you through feeding and vaccines.
Don't sayYour pet is at risk! Book now before it's too late!
SM · Supermarket
EnergeticValue-drivenWelcoming

Lead with what's fresh and what's good value. This is where warm Nigerian colloquial touches belong.

SayFresh tomatoes just landed. Come and pick the best before Saturday!
SayẸ kú àbọ̀! Your weekly basket, sorted in one stop.
Don't sayLOWEST PRICES IN NIGERIA!!!

Bilingual and local language rules

ContextEnglishYoruba, Pidgin, colloquial
Supermarket ads, social posts, in-store signsPrimaryWelcome. Greetings (Ẹ kú àbọ̀, Ẹ ṣé), light Pidgin and local references.
Vet marketing, remindersPrimaryGreetings and warmth only. Keep care instructions in plain English.
Pharmacy marketingPrimaryGreetings only.
Medical content: dosage, warnings, labels, leaflets, prescriptions, consult notesAlwaysNever. If a translation is needed, use a full professional translation checked by a pharmacist, not a colloquial paraphrase.
Receipts, invoices, legal and payment textAlwaysNever.

Write it right

  • Always write Yoruba with its tone marks and dot-below letters (Ẹ kú àbọ̀, not "E ku abo").
  • Have a native speaker check any Yoruba copy before it is printed.
  • Write prices as ₦12,500. Don't use N12,500 or NGN in customer copy.

Health claims

  • Never promise a cure or a result.
  • Do not advertise prescription-only medicines to the public. Talk about the service ("Our pharmacist can review your prescription").
  • Have pharmacy advertising checked against current NAFDAC and PCN rules.

House style

  • Robakeye in running text, ROBAKEYE only in the logo.
  • Sentence case for headings and buttons.
  • Write "the vet clinic", "the pharmacy", "the supermarket" in lower case.
  • Keep exclamation marks for the supermarket, and use at most one.

Rules for paper

  • Thermal receipts print in black only. Use the single-colour badge and wordmark, never the colour artwork, which prints as grey mush.
  • Set receipt text in Work Sans with tabular figures so prices line up. Most thermal printers need the header as a 1-bit bitmap: export it from robakeye-primary-navy.svg at 576 px wide in pure black.
  • Mark pharmacy lines on mixed receipts with "PH item" and the dispensing pharmacist.
  • A4 invoices use the horizontal logo, a Navy and Amber rule, and Navy text.

Before this goes to print

  1. Confirm the circle colour with the owner. This system keeps the bright Badge Blue from the artwork rather than the navy circle described in the brief.
  2. Get a printed proof of Badge Blue and Amber on your actual stock, and record the Pantone matches from your printer in this guide.
  3. Find out what RPL stands for and whether it should stay in the mark. It is kept for continuity, and the icon version already drops it.
  4. Have a native speaker check all Yoruba copy, and have a pharmacist check pharmacy advertising rules before launch.
  5. Book a shoot at the Ologuneru flagship using the shot lists in section 06, with signed releases.
